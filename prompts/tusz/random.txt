[Task Definition]
From a professional EEG diagnostician perspective, classify the provided electroencephalogram (EEG) time-series plot of Test EEG Image into one of three categories: "NON-SEIZURE", "FOCAL SEIZURE", or "GENERALIZED SEIZURE".

[Diagnostic Criteria]
1. NON-SEIZURE (Background/Artifact):
   - Background: Chaotic, quasi-random fluctuations without organized rhythmic structure.
   - Isolated Artifacts: Eye blinks or muscle noise that are single events (not rhythmic).
   - Postictal Slowing: If the signal is just slow waves (1-3 Hz) without spikes, classify as Non-Seizure (unless distinct spike-and-wave is present).

2. SEIZURE:
   Look for a "Distinct Departure" from background defined by:
   - Morphology:
     * Spike-and-Slow-Wave: A sharp spike (<0.1s) followed by a slow wave (2–10 Hz).
     * Polyspikes: Multiple fast spikes followed by a slow wave.
     * High Amplitude: Signal clearly taller than the background activity.
   - Synchrony: Rhythmic patterns appearing simultaneously (within 0.2s) across multiple channels.
   - Frequency Constraint: Repetitive waves typically fall between 2 Hz (Delta) and 10 Hz (Alpha).
   - Special Cases: Intense, obscuring muscle artifact that flattens/overwhelms cerebral activity can indicate Tonic-Clonic seizure.

3. SEIZURE TYPE, by the spatial extent of the seizure activity:
   - FOCAL SEIZURE: The seizure activity is confined to, or clearly maximal over, the channels of one hemisphere or one region (left: Fp1-F7, F7-T3, T3-T5, T5-O1, Fp1-F3, F3-C3, C3-P3, P3-O1; right: Fp2-F8, F8-T4, T4-T6, T6-O2, Fp2-F4, F4-C4, C4-P4, P4-O2), while the other channels show background activity or a weaker version of it.
   - GENERALIZED SEIZURE: The seizure activity appears at the same time and with similar morphology over both hemispheres, including the midline channels (Fz-Cz, Cz-Pz).

[Analysis Protocol]
Before categorizing, generate a brief visual description of the Test EEG Image to ground the reasoning:
1. Frequency Check: Is the rhythmic activity within 2–10 Hz (Seizure) or random/chaotic (Non-Seizure)?
2. Morphology Check: Are there specific "Spike-and-Wave" complexes or just fuzzy noise?
3. Synchrony Check: Is the pattern visible across multiple channels at the same time?
4. Extent Check: If seizure activity is present, is it confined to one hemisphere or region (Focal), or present over both hemispheres and the midline (Generalized)?
